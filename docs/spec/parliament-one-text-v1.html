The room as one text

C311, piece 2. Your text, each prompt you sent and each reply become one text in one editor, so a selection runs across all of it and can be typed over, struck or deleted. A prompt sent shows as p> and its words in the prompt colour. A reply is a quote block. Nothing here is built, and nothing stored changes.

Piece 1 is built (prompts in the text follow the bar). Every section below draws one decision; section 10 lists what to rule.

4 · Deleting a whole prompt or reply

A part selected whole and deleted is the delete you have now (Enter twice, 20 seconds to bring it back), reached from the text. It is not an edit: the part leaves the chat, and a model is no longer sent it.

  • A source's list of results: each result is the source's record, with a link. It can be deleted whole or brought into a prompt (include:), not rewritten.
  • The line of where words came from (source, page, revision, licence; or the model and the day). It stays with its quote however the words are edited, and reads "edited by you" once they are. Deleting the quote deletes its line.
  • A reply still arriving: fixed until it has finished, then text like the rest. Typing elsewhere in the room goes on meanwhile.

7 · Ctrl+A, and other keys that reach far

In one editor, Ctrl+A would select the whole chat, and one Backspace after it would strike or delete everything. That slip has already happened on a single reply (C301 (e)).

KeyDoes
Ctrl+A, onceSelects the part the caret is in: your text, one prompt, or one reply.
Ctrl+A, againSelects the whole text. The line under the bar says "All of 202 is selected".
Backspace at the start of a partStops there. It does not join a reply to its prompt, or a prompt to your text. To join, select across and delete.
Enter in a promptDoes nothing, as now: a prompt is one line of its own.
Ctrl+Enter anywhereWhat Enter in the bar would do, as now.
Ctrl+ZTakes back the last change in the text, across parts, as one step. undo: does the same from the bar.

9 · Stored, and what each act writes

Nothing new is stored and nothing is migrated. The editor is one; the messages under it stay the records they are, and every backup reads as before. An older Parliament opening the same data draws its cards as it always did.

You doWritten
Type or delete within a partThat message's blocks, and an edit record where a model has read it. As now.
Delete or type over across partsThe same, once for each message touched, in one transaction, so one undo.
Delete a part wholeThe message is deleted as delete: deletes one now, with its 20 seconds.
Words left over from a deleted partThey become words of the part before, as typed there.
Change a sent promptAn edit record on that prompt. Its content, what was sent, stays as sent.
Edit a source's quoteAn edit record, waiting for a model; its source (page, revision, licence) untouched.
  • Branches: the editor shows the branch on screen. A prompt above several branches is one line with the branch tabs under it, as now; each branch holds its own copy (ruled 16 November).
  • A long chat: one editor over hundreds of messages is to be measured before building. If it is slow, parts far from the screen are drawn as plain text until scrolled to.
  • A screen reader is told each part as it is entered: "your text", "your prompt", "reply from Wikipedia".
  • A phone: long-press selection across parts needs trying on one.

10 · To rule

Replaces

  • C280 (d): a selection across cards is to copy only.
  • C214 (a), (b): a prompt and its reply typed over together join and fill the bar.
  • C301 (f): "a prompt typed in the bar stays as it is", and one text of your own per room.
  • The prompt's bubble, which becomes a line of p> and its words.

To rule

  1. One text (section 2): a prompt sent as p> and its words in vermillion, no bubble; a reply as a quote block with a grey rule, a source and a model alike, the line under it saying who.
  2. Struck where a model has read, gone where none has (section 3).
  3. A part selected whole is deleted with two presses and 20 seconds to bring it back (section 4).
  4. Three fixed things: a source's list of results, the line of where words came from, a reply still arriving (section 5).
  5. A changed prompt is an edit and sends nothing; again: asks it as it now reads (section 6).
  6. Ctrl+A in two steps, and Backspace stopping at the start of a part (section 7).
  7. Marks stay within one part; across parts they are refused with a line (section 8).
  8. Order of building: the one editor with nothing else changed (selection, typing and striking across parts) first; then deleting a part from the text; then the look (p> and quote blocks). Or the look first, since it is what you asked to see.
