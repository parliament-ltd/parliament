A room to write in

C301 (f), proposed. The first screen is already an empty room (C302). Here it becomes a page you can type into: your own text, never sent by itself, marked like any reply, with prompts written in it and answered in it. A prompt typed in the bar still starts a chat as now. Your text is one new kind of message, shown in full in section 5 before anything is built.

The empty room as it is now, with one line changed: a click in it puts the caret there. The bar keeps the focus when the page opens, so ? and commands work at once, as now.

2 · Writing, and marking it

What you write is yours, and is never sent by itself. Everything a reply takes, it takes: headings and lists as you type them, a comment (Ctrl+Alt+M), a highlight, a prompt in the text (Ctrl+Alt+P), "as a reply" on a prompt's row. Until a model has read it, what you type is simply your text: nothing is marked as an edit, and nothing is numbered.

3 · The answer, in your text

Each prompt in the text comes back where it was asked, as the model's suggestion, to accept or reject. With nothing typed, the model is told to answer only in those places, so a reply that holds nothing else is one line under your text, not a card. A prompt "as a reply", or words you typed, still bring a reply, under it.

4 · Once a model has read it

From the first answer on, your text is a message the model has read, and changes to it are your edits, as on any reply or prompt: marked, and told once with the next prompt (C216), so what the model said about it still makes sense. Suggesting holds them back, as it does elsewhere.

5 · What is stored: shown before building

Your text is a message on your side of the chat, as a prompt is, with one new field saying it is your text and not a prompt. Everything else it holds, a message holds already.

A prompt, now

  conversationId: "c-201a…",
  role: "user",
  content: "tighten the whole thing",
  tokenCount: 0,
  incomplete: false,
  createdAt: "…"
}

Your text

{
  id: "m-5e1a…",
  conversationId: "c-212…",
  role: "user",
  writing: true,
  content: "# Committee notes\n\nFirst, the budget…",
  blocks: [ { type: "heading", level: 1, … },
            { type: "paragraph", … }, … ],
  tokenCount: 0,
  incomplete: false,
  createdAt: "…"
}
  • writing: optional, true for your own text. A message without it reads as now. Its words go to a model only with a prompt, under the lead line section 3 shows.
  • content and blocks are a message's own fields: blocks is the form a reply keeps once edited in place (C212 (b)), and content its words. Until a model has read your text, both follow what you type; after, content stays as it was read and blocks carries your edits, as for any message.
  • Whether a model has read it is not stored: it has once a model's reply follows it in its chat. An answer from a source does not count.
  • Its chat is an ordinary chat. No migration: no record is rewritten, and no store, index or database version changes. A restore checks writing (true, or not there). Backups carry it from this version on; one made before reads as now.
  • An older Parliament restoring a newer backup drops writing, and shows your text as a prompt of yours that had no reply, its marks intact.
  • Drafting Desk's documents, the earlier room for writing your own (R39), are untouched: they stay in their own store, readable in Drafting Desk, hidden as ruled (C288 (a)). Copying one into a room to write in can come with R39, after the refactor.
  • Emptied again, your text's chat stays, empty, until delete:212: nothing you made is removed for you.

6 · To rule

  1. The stored change: one optional field on a message, writing: true, marking it as your own text rather than a prompt, as section 5 shows. Nothing migrated.
  2. A draft until a model reads it: before the first answer, what you type is simply your text, in Editing and in Suggesting; after, your changes are edits like any others, marked and told once.
  3. With nothing typed, answers only in the text: the model is told to write nothing beyond its answers, so a reply of answers alone is one line under your text. This holds in every room when nothing is typed.
  4. Ctrl+Enter in your text does what Enter in the bar would, the line under the bar saying what first.
  5. One text per room, its chat's first message; later prompts in the bar go on below it. Writing more of your own further down can come later.
