Indent and align, v1

Your two questions from testing, as one change to how a line of text is kept. Tab moves a line in, Shift+Tab out, up to three steps; a list's item becomes a sub-item. A line can sit at the left, in the centre or at the right. Both are said from the bar too, for a phone or for a whole selection.

Today neither exists: Tab moves between a table's cells, and anywhere else it leaves the text; lists are one level; nothing is centred.

1 · In your text

A draft in a room, as it would look. Headings, lines, lists and checklists all take both.

2 · The keys

Indent
Tabthe line, or every line selected, one step in; a list's item becomes a sub-item, up to three
Shift+Tabone step out
Tab in a tablethe next cell, as now
Esc, then Tableaves the text, for the next thing on the page, so a keyboard alone can still move on
Backspace at a line's startone step out, before it joins the line above
Align
Ctrl+Shift+Ecentre
Ctrl+Shift+Rright
Ctrl+Shift+Lleft again

⌘⇧E, R and L on a Mac. The lines the caret or the selection is in; a new line after Enter keeps its line's place.

3 · From the bar

For a phone, which has no Tab, and for many lines at once. They act on the text last in hand, as strikethrough: does.

COMMAND>align:center
Enter centres the 2 lines selected in 211
COMMAND>align:right
Enter puts the line with the caret in 211 at the right
COMMAND>indent:
Enter moves the 3 lines selected in 211 one step in
COMMAND>indent:off
Enter moves them one step out
  • align:left takes a line back to the left, as Ctrl+Shift+L does. undo: is not needed: Ctrl+Z in the text takes either back.
  • The "/" menu gains nothing: these change how a line sits, not what kind of line it is.

4 · What it is, and what a model reads

  • Not an edit. Moving a line in or centring it changes how it sits, not its words: it is never struck or marked, in a draft or in a model's reply, and it goes into no prompt as an edit. Like ticking a checklist's box.
  • A model reads the words, and the lists' levels. A sub-item goes as a list inside a list, as Markdown writes it. A plain line moved in, and any alignment, are not sent: Markdown has no way to say them, and four spaces before a line would turn it into code.
  • Copied or saved as Markdown: the same. In a backup, all of it is kept.
  • Right to left: in Arabic or Hebrew text, "in" is from the right, and "left" and "right" are the page's own.

5 · What is stored: shown before building

Two fields on a line of a message's text (its blocks), each left out when it is the usual: no migration, and an older Parliament shows the line as before.

blocks: [
  { id: "b-31…", type: "heading", level: 2, text: "Moving plan", align: "center" },
  { id: "b-32…", type: "list_item", kind: "bullet", text: "Empty the cupboards", indent: 1 },
  { id: "b-33…", type: "list_item", kind: "check", checked: false, text: "Ask about the lift", indent: 1 },
  { id: "b-34…", type: "paragraph", text: "Anna", align: "right" }
]
  • indent: 1, 2 or 3, absent at 0. align: "center" or "right", absent at the left. On a paragraph, a heading, a list's item, a checklist's item and a quote; not on a table or a chart.
  • A restore checks both: any other value, and the field is dropped, the words kept.

6 · To rule

  1. Tab and Shift+Tab move a line, or the lines selected, in and out, up to three steps; a list's item becomes a sub-item; in a table, the next cell as now; Esc then Tab leaves the text.
  2. Ctrl+Shift+E, R and L centre, right and left again.
  3. From the bar: align:center, align:right, align:left, indent: and indent:off.
  4. Not an edit, and a model reads only the words and the lists' levels.
  5. Stored as above: indent and align on a line.
