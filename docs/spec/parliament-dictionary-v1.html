Dictionary, v1

A dictionary and a thesaurus as one board, opened from the bar: dictionary:reluctant on what the word means, thesaurus:reluctant on words that mean the same. Its words come from lists shipped with Parliament and looked up on this computer: nothing is sent, nothing costs, and it works offline. English first, as ruled.

And sums, which models get wrong: calc: answered on the line under the bar, and figures you select added up there. No board for them.

1 · The board

The word, what it means by kind, and beside it the words that mean the same, and its opposite where the lists know one. A word on the board is a click: it looks that word up, or, with a word selected in your text, puts it in its place.

  • Looked-up words are not kept: no history, nothing in Records. A list of your own words, with notes, could come later.

2 · From your text

Select a word, in a reply or in what you write, and look it up without leaving it: dictionary: with nothing after it takes the selection, and so does a key, Ctrl+Alt+L ("look up"). A word clicked on the board then takes the selection's place, as an edit, marked as your edits are.

Selected in your text
your text, 213The board was reluctant to fund a second year without last year's figures.
COMMAND>dictionary:
Enter looks up "reluctant", selected in 213 · Ctrl+Alt+L does the same
"hesitant" clicked on the board
your text, 213The board was reluctant hesitant to fund a second year without last year's figures.

An edit like any other: undo: takes it back. In a draft it is made without marks, as your drafts are, unless Suggesting is on.

  • Not read from what is typed in the text itself: the text cannot tell your typing from pasted text, and text from outside never runs a command. The selection and the bar are yours alone.
  • A word the lists do not have is answered with the nearest they do: dictionary:recieve, "No "recieve" in the word lists. Did you mean receive?". A misspelt command, thesarus:, gets the bar's own "Did you mean thesaurus:?".
  • In Swedish, Finnish, Japanese or Chinese the board says its words are English for now.

3 · Kept light

Nothing until asked. No checking of what you type, no list loaded on the first screen. The board's own code loads the first time it opens.
A piece at a time. The lists are split by the first letters of a word. Looking up "reluctant" fetches the "re" piece from Parliament's own site, a small file, and nothing else.
Kept after. The browser keeps each piece, as it keeps Parliament, so a word looked up twice, or offline, needs nothing fetched.
Off the page's own thread. A piece is read and searched beside the page, so typing and scrolling never wait for it.
  • The lists ship in the release archive and are hashed with everything else. Their size is measured when built, and said here before it ships.
  • No new host, and the security policy does not change: the pieces come from Parliament's own address.

4 · Sums, on the line under the bar

A sum written in the bar is worked out as you type, and the line under it says what it comes to. Figures selected in a reply are added up there too, as a spreadsheet says what its selection adds up to. Nothing opens.

Written
COMMAND>calc:48000*2/12
= 8,000 · Enter puts it in the bar's history
COMMAND>calc:(1250+3400)*1.25
= 5,812.50
Selected in a reply
Sonnet 5.5, 204Staff 31,000, materials 9,500, and outreach 7,500, for 48,000 a year.
3 figures selected: sum 48,000 · average 16,000
  • Worked out by a small reader of sums written for Parliament, exact with decimals: + − × ÷, brackets, percentages and powers. The security policy forbids running text as code, so nothing typed is ever run.
  • Figures are read as the reply's language writes them: 31,000 and 31 000 and 31.000,50 each as meant.

5 · To rule

  1. One board, two commands: dictionary: on the meanings, thesaurus: on the same meaning; the two halves side by side, or as tabs when narrow.
  2. From your text: the selection, with dictionary: alone or Ctrl+Alt+L (⌘⌥L on a Mac, to be tried there, where a browser may keep it for itself); a word clicked takes its place as an edit.
  3. The lists: Open English WordNet, shipped, fetched a piece at a time, nothing sent; the size said before it ships.
  4. Looked-up words not kept; your own word list later.
  5. Sums: calc: on the line under the bar, and a selection's sum there; no calculator board.
